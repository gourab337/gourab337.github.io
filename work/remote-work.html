When the model isn't the bottleneck

Predicting how 32,453 developers work, and finding where the limit really is.

Remote, in the office, one of two kinds of hybrid, or fully flexible: can you predict a developer's work arrangement from where they live, who they work for and what they do? We set up a strict evaluation, compared five model families, then ran 74 more configurations to see how far the score could go. They all stopped at the same place. The charts below show what that ceiling looks like and what the model learned along the way.

About the code. This was a two-person project, so the code stays private; I can walk through it in a discussion. The data is the public 2025 Stack Overflow Developer Survey (Open Database License). Hover over any chart for exact values.

74 configurations, one ceiling

Best cross-validated Macro-F1 of each configuration, grouped by model family (5 of the shared training folds).

Gradient boosting, stacking, ordinal models, neural nets, TabPFN, AutoML and Bayesian models all land between 0.25 and 0.37. When very different learners converge like that, the limit is in the inputs. The most likely missing variable is the employer's own office policy, which the survey never asks about.

Why we didn't trust accuracy

Cross-validated means over the same 10 resamples.

Switch to accuracy and the random forest looks second best. It gets there by almost never predicting the rare "fully flexible" class (3.9% recall). Macro-F1 weights every class equally, so it exposes that.

Same folds, every time

Macro-F1 on each of the 10 shared CV folds.

LightGBM on the full survey beat the 8-predictor multinomial on all 10 folds, by 0.032 on average. The folds overlap, so that is consistent evidence, not 10 independent wins.

Where the final model gets confused

In-person and remote are recognised best. The two hybrid classes and fully flexible leak into every other class, so the five arrangements are not a clean scale from office to home.

What moves the odds of remote vs in-person

Odds ratios from the final multinomial, other predictors held fixed (log scale).

Freelancers and developers in Ukraine are far more likely to be remote. The Netherlands and India are much less likely to be fully remote. Associations in a self-selected sample, not causal effects.

What made it rigorous

  • No leakage. All grouping, imputation, clipping and scaling were learned inside each training fold, never on the full data.
  • One look at the test set. The model was chosen on cross-validation and scored on the test set exactly once. LightGBM came later, so it is reported on CV only, even though it scored higher.
  • A metric that fits the problem. Five imbalanced classes, so Macro-F1, plus class weighting (0.298 → 0.330) rather than throwing data away by downsampling.
  • Shared resamples. Every model was compared on the same 10 folds, so differences come from the models, not from luck in the split.
  • Honest scope. Interpretations are associations. The sample is self-selected, so the results describe survey respondents, not all developers.

Stack: R (caret, recipes, glmnet, ranger, e1071, lightgbm), Python for the extended experiments. Built in 2026 at the University of Sydney.
